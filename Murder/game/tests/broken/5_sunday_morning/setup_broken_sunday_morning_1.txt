Chapter: An Empty Manor
Character: Thomas Moody
Dawn has come by the time I wake, stiff in my watch chair.
I cannot believe I fell asleep.
I check my mask at once, and find it slightly crooked.
I adjust it and look about the room, but nobody is watching me.
I turn to Captain Sinha, who is sitting perfectly straight on his chair.
Sushil Sinha: Ah, Mr Moody, you are awake.
Sushil Sinha: I believe you managed a little sleep.
Sushil Sinha: I did not, but it is quite all right.
Sushil Sinha: Do not worry, nothing happened during the night.
Thomas Moody: Good.
Thomas Moody: I am sorry, I did not think I would fall asleep.
Sushil Sinha: It happens.
Sushil Sinha: Do not blame yourself too much for it.
I look around the room.
Everyone is awake now, all of them dishevelled.
Doctor Baldwin is in particularly bad shape, having contracted a sort of fever.
Samuel Manning is already back to drinking from his pocket flask.
The ladies have managed to make themselves presentable, and Captain Sinha looks as though he had passed a perfectly ordinary night in his own bed.
I wonder what I look like, but it hardly matters now.
It is time for action, and Captain Sinha takes the lead.
Sushil Sinha: Very well, now that everyone is awake, I think it is time we planned our next move.
People murmur in agreement.
Sushil Sinha: Good.
Sushil Sinha: What I suggest is this.
Sushil Sinha: Let us go quickly to our rooms and get ready for the long walk.
Sushil Sinha: We can meet afterwards in the entrance hall.
Sushil Sinha: The sooner we leave, the better.
Amelia Baxter: Captain, Miss Marsh and I have been speaking quietly together these last few minutes.
Amelia Baxter: And we have come to the sorry conclusion that we shall never make it.
Sushil Sinha: Not make it, Miss Baxter?
Sushil Sinha: It is a long journey, I will admit, but not a difficult one, merely a matter of following the road.
Sushil Sinha: And we shall walk slowly, and stop as often as you need.
Amelia Baxter: I do not doubt it is only a stroll to a man of your training, Captain.
Amelia Baxter: But my constitution is not the thing it once was, and I am not dressed for such an undertaking.
Rosalind Marsh: And I am no better.
Rosalind Marsh: My health has never been strong, and a whole day on a wet road would be very taxing.
Captain Sinha considers this, then turns towards me.
Sushil Sinha: Perhaps they are right, Mr Moody.
Sushil Sinha: This journey might not be fit for the ladies.
Sushil Sinha: It would be best if they stayed here.
Sushil Sinha: And even the men are not in perfect shape.
He looks at Samuel Manning, and Doctor Baldwin.
They both look sickly and exhausted.
Sushil Sinha: We would certainly go faster, just the two of us.
Sushil Sinha: We could fetch help and be back before sundown.
Sushil Sinha: What do you think?
I do not like it, but I cannot think of a strong argument against his idea.
Thomas Moody: Very well, Captain.
Thomas Moody: The two of us, then.
Thomas Moody: I assume Doctor Baldwin and Mr Manning will stay here to protect the ladies.
Samuel Manning is still deep in thought.
But Doctor Baldwin gives a slight nod in agreement.
They will not be the best bodyguards today, but that will have to do.
Thomas Moody: The rest of you keep together, with the door shut until we come back.
Thomas Moody: Together, mind.
Thomas Moody: Nobody wanders this house alone, not for a moment, not for any reason.
Rosalind Marsh: We shall stay here for as long as needed.
Rosalind Marsh: Do not worry.
Sushil Sinha: Very well.
Sushil Sinha: No need to waste any more time, then.
Sushil Sinha: Mr Moody.
Thomas Moody: Yes, let us go.
We take our coats, leave the billiard room, and put Claythorn Manor behind us.
