Chapter: An Empty Manor
Character: Lady Claythorn
I wake in my clothes, with the light already coming through the curtains.
The house is eerly quiet.
No footsteps below. No fires being laid. No trays.
For a brief moment that seems odd to me, then I remember.
The staff is gone, we are on our own.
Captain Sinha is in the chair by the door.
His chin is on his chest, the revolver lies in his lap, and he is fast asleep.
So much for the watch.
I stand up, and the noise wakes him up.
Sushil Sinha: Forgive me.
Sushil Sinha: I closed my eyes for a moment.
He puts the revolver back into his pocket without looking at it, and he straightens his coat.
Lady Claythorn: Do not worry.
Lady Claythorn: Nobody came to my door in the night.
Lady Claythorn: That is all that matters.
Sushil Sinha: Right.
Sushil Sinha: Well, I guess now there is daylight, we can leave.
Sushil Sinha: That has not changed since last night.
Sushil Sinha: The question is how.
Lady Claythorn: The town is a long way.
Lady Claythorn: We came up from the station on Friday, and it took the car the better part of an hour.
Sushil Sinha: Ten miles, then. Perhaps more.
Sushil Sinha: On foot, and on that road, it is the whole of the afternoon.
Sushil Sinha: We could do it, though it is not ideal.
Lady Claythorn: There is an old tourer in the garage.
Lady Claythorn: But I have no idea if it is still working.
Lady Claythorn: I know nothing about motors.
Sushil Sinha: I know a little.
Sushil Sinha: We could check it then, see if we can start it.
Lady Claythorn: Another thing.
Lady Claythorn: What shall we do about Mr Manning?
Sushil Sinha: Right.
Sushil Sinha: I locked him up in his room last night.
Sushil Sinha: It would not be fair to let him in there.
Sushil Sinha: We should at least open his door before we go.
Lady Claythorn: Alright, so we can sort those things out before deciding on our next move.
Sushil Sinha: Ver well.
Sushil Sinha: One more note, we shouldn't say anything to anybody until we are out of this house.
Sushil Sinha: If one of them is behind this, I do not want to see their face when they learn what we know.
Lady Claythorn: Then we keep away from them.
Sushil Sinha: As long as we can.
So that is the plan.
A few things to check and then we are out of here.
I put my shoes on, and we go out into the corridor together.
The doors are all shut.
Nothing moves.
Sushil Sinha: Please, lead the way.
Map Choice: Scullery
Dirty dishes from Saturday night, and the smell of wet stone.
Lady Claythorn: Captain, on Friday I noticed a bottle of rat poison standing on that shelf, open.
Lady Claythorn: I did not think much of it at the time.
Lady Claythorn: But now, I do not know.
Sushil Sinha: You think it could have been used against Mr Moody?
Lady Claythorn: Perhaps.
Lady Claythorn: I am not sure of anything any more.
Sushil Sinha: If it was, then this weekend was arranged for something terrible.
Sushil Sinha: That is one more reason to be extremely cautious.
Lady Claythorn: Of course.
There is nothing else for us here.
Map Choice: Garden
The gravel is empty, and the tracks of the car go off down the drive in the wet.
A mist hangs over the lawn.
At the bottom of the garden stands a small shed.
We walk down to it.
I try to open it but it is locked.
Sushil Sinha: Let me try the key the butler gave me yesterday.
The key turns, and we go in.
A workbench, a coil of rope, tools that have not been touched in years.
And in the middle of the floor, a metal petrol tin catches my eye.
The Captain unscrews the cap and puts his nose to it.
Sushil Sinha: It is petrol, and it is half full.
Sushil Sinha: If we had a car, there would be more than enough to reach the town.
Lady Claythorn: Good, let us keep searching in that case.
Map Choice: Garage
The garage holds an assortment of discarded things from the house.
The old tourer is still here, under its dust.
The Captain lifts the bonnet and puts his head under it.
I stand back and try to look as though I know what he is doing.
Sushil Sinha: The engine seems to be in order.
He gets in and tries the starter.
It turns over once and dies.
Sushil Sinha: Dry.
Sushil Sinha: There is not a drop in the tank.
Sushil Sinha: Without petrol it is no use to us.
Sushil Sinha: But there may be some elsewhere on the estate.
He closes the bonnet gently, and we leave the car as it was.
Map Choice: Library
The book is still open on the table, at the page I read on Friday.
Kilbraith.
I close it. Whoever comes here next need not find it so easily.
Map Choice: Portrait Gallery
A dozen Claythorns in their frames, looking down the gallery at nobody.
They were here before this weekend, and they will be here when we are gone.
Not one of them wears my face, and this morning I am glad of it.
Map Choice: Butler's Room
The attic stair.
On Friday every door up here was locked against me, and I had no key.
This morning the Captain has two.
His room.
The bed is stripped and the wardrobe is empty. He took his own things, at least.
Against the far wall stands a cabinet with an iron band across its doors.
The master key opens it.
The candlesticks from the dining table. The salver. The good spoons.
A flat case, and inside it the pearls I wore on Friday night.
Everything the house was dressed with for this weekend, packed and ready, and left behind.
Sushil Sinha: He left in a hurry.
Lady Claythorn: No.
Lady Claythorn: He had the whole evening to load that car, and he had this key.
Lady Claythorn: He did not leave it. He means to come back for it.
The Captain closes the cabinet and turns the key in it again.
Sushil Sinha: Then we had better not be here when he does.
Map Choice: Storage Room
Trunks, mostly empty.
A spare livery on a hanger, and a hamper with the name of a London theatrical supplier on the lid.
The whole weekend came up here in these boxes, and it will not be going back in them.
Map Choice: Male Servants Room
The footman's room, stripped to the bed frame.
He was the best of us, I think. Not one word out of place all weekend.
I hope he is a long way from here.
Map Choice: Female Servants Room
The girl's room.
A hairbrush on the washstand, forgotten in the hurry.
She was the youngest of us, and the most frightened, and she had the least to be frightened of.
I hope she is a long way from here too.
Map Choice: Tea room
The fire is dead, and there is nothing of interest here.
Voices, somewhere on the other side of the hall.
Ted Harring: Hello? Is anyone there?
Mr Harring, and Miss Baxter answering him, too low for me to catch the words.
The Captain's hand closes on my arm, and we stand very still behind the door until the voices go up the stair.
Sushil Sinha: I can hear Ted Harring and Amelia Baxter.
Sushil Sinha: They are probably trying to understand where everyone is.
Lady Claythorn: Maybe, but we should not take any chances.
Lady Claythorn: Not yet, at least.
Sushil Sinha: You are right, better to avoid them for the time being.
Map Choice: Kitchen
The kitchen is empty.
But the food that was meant to be prepared today is still lying on the counter.
Lady Claythorn: We do not know how long the journey will take, so we should take some provisions.
Sushil Sinha: Good idea.
Sushil Sinha: Let us take enough to get us through the afternoon.
I put bread and some cheese into a basket, with a bottle of water, in case it proves useful later.
We have been through the house for the better part of the morning.
The Captain looks at his watch, and we go back to the hall.
There is no time left for anything else.
