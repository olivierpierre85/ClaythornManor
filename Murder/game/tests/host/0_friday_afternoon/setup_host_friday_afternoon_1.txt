Chapter: Introduction
Character: Lady Claythorn
The journey from London is finally coming to a close.
I am eager, but also nervous, about the most unusual role I have ever been hired to play.
The hostess of a Scottish manor.
A prominent lady.
All of it to entertain a group of unlikely people.
I received only the barest information about the reason behind the whole thing.
I was told I would learn more once I was there.
Well, here I am.
Let's make the most of it.
I have barely stepped down from the carriage when an old acquaintance in a butler's uniform approaches me.
Butler: Good, you made it on time.
Butler: Please come this way.
Without ceremony, he guides me into a car.
Butler: We will be there soon.
Butler: Claythorn Manor.
Butler: From now on you are the lady of this place.
Butler: Don't forget it.
Lady Claythorn: I don't understand why you can't be the lord.
Lady Claythorn: You would clearly be better at it than I.
Butler: You mean because I am the better actor?
Lady Claythorn: I didn't say that.
Lady Claythorn: And you are not.
Lady Claythorn: But I know you are familiar with that type of house.
Butler: True, I was a footman when I was younger.
Butler: A very long time ago.
Butler: But that's precisely why I should be the butler.
Butler: You see, in this kind of environment, the staff are scrutinised more than the host.
Butler: That's a rule.
Butler: As the lady of the house, you can do almost anything.
Butler: At worst you will be labelled as eccentric.
Butler: But to maintain the illusion, the staff can't afford a mistake.
Butler: That's why I have to be in charge of them.
Butler: Besides, a butler must always be a man.
Lady Claythorn: Right.
Lady Claythorn: But I've been assured that the guests we are receiving won't be accustomed to all this.
Lady Claythorn: None of them is of a standing important enough to notice little mistakes in the service.
Butler: Maybe so, but the rest of our staff are all actors too.
Butler: They have very little domestic experience, if any.
Butler: So we shouldn't take any chances.
Finally, I can spot the Manor from afar.
This is becoming real now.
I feel a knot inside my stomach and wonder what I have got myself into.
Butler: All right, the others are already there to prepare the house.
Butler: Let us not waste any time.
Two other people are gathered in the entrance hall.
A young man, rather dashing, and a girl who cannot look more than eighteen years old.
Lady Claythorn: Is that everyone?
Butler: I am afraid so.
Butler: It would have been dangerous to bring more people in.
Lady Claythorn: Is that enough for a house this size?
Butler: Normally no, but for the weekend we shall have to make it work.
Butler: I have already briefed them, so they know what their roles are.
Butler: This gentleman will be acting as footman and driver.
Footman: Nice to meet you, ma'am.
He says it with deference, as though he is already in character.
Good.
Lady Claythorn: Hello.
Lady Claythorn: What should I call you?
Footman: Thomas, my lady.
Butler: And this is Elsie, our cook and maid for the weekend.
Young woman: Hello, ma'am.
Butler: Those are not their real names, of course.
Lady Claythorn: Of course.
I will not know their names, and they won't know mine.
And if I run into them on a stage in the future, we will act as if we were meeting for the first time.
Those were the terms we agreed on.
Lady Claythorn: And what should I call you?
Butler: Hmm, with any luck you won't have to call me at all, since I'll be the one managing everything this weekend.
Butler: But if you must, call me 'Barrow'.
Lady Claythorn: Barrow it is, then.
Butler: Very well, now that the introductions have been made, I believe everybody knows what their role will be this weekend.
Butler: Now let's get to it.
Footman: Yes, sir!
Young woman: Very well.
They both head downstairs.
As for me, I go to my room to settle in and get ready for the evening.
